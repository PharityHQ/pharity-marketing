Who can prescribe in a wellness program?

Only a licensed clinician, acting on their own judgment, for a patient they have evaluated, in the state that patient is in. Everything else in a program is arranged around that one fact.

By the Pharity team Reviewed October 3, 2026 General information, not legal advice

The direct answer. A physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant, licensed in the state where the patient is located, acting within their scope and on their own clinical judgment, after evaluating that specific patient. Not a business, not a brand, and not a contract.

Two conditions that are easy to miss

The licence follows the patient, not the business

Telehealth is generally governed by where the patient is at the time of the visit, not where the clinician or the company sits. A program operating across several states needs clinicians licensed in each of them. This is the single most common structural gap in an otherwise well-built program, and it is also the easiest to check before launch.

The decision has to be real

An evaluation that cannot result in "no" is not an evaluation. A program whose marketing implies approval is likely, or whose economics depend on a high prescribe rate, puts pressure on exactly the judgment that has to stay independent. A clinician declining to prescribe should be an ordinary outcome that costs the business nothing.

Where the pharmacy fits

A prescription is dispensed by a licensed pharmacy, which ships directly to the patient. For compounded preparations that is typically a 503A pharmacy compounding for an identified patient against a specific prescription, or a 503B outsourcing facility. The pharmacy holds its own licence for every state it ships into, and that licence is as state-bound as the clinician's.

Nobody between the pharmacy and the patient should be taking possession of the medication. Receiving a drug that is not for your own use is distribution, and distribution is separately licensed.

What this means for each kind of business

A clinic or med spa with its own prescriber

Your clinician prescribes in their own name, for patients they evaluate, in states where they are licensed. What you need is reach: a way to get a prescription to a pharmacy licensed in your patient's state, priced and tracked without a portal for each one.

A gym, studio or brand with no prescriber

You do not prescribe and should not try to. The clinicians come from a professional entity that owns them. You market, you own the member relationship, and you are paid for your own program at your own flat price.

A pharmacy

You dispense against prescriptions written by clinicians you do not employ and did not influence, for patients in states where you hold a licence.

The sentence to test any program against

If you cannot say plainly who evaluated this patient, under which licence, in which state, and that they could have said no without it costing anyone anything, the program is not structured correctly yet. Everything else is detail by comparison.

This article is general information rather than legal advice. Scope of practice, supervision and telehealth rules are set by state law and differ meaningfully between them.

Common questions

Can a nurse practitioner prescribe in a wellness program?
In many states yes, within their scope and subject to that state collaboration or supervision rules, which vary considerably. Whether a given NP may prescribe a given therapy in a given state is a question for that state board.
Does the clinician need a licence in the patient state?
Generally yes. Telehealth is usually governed by where the patient is located at the time of the visit, so a program covering several states needs clinicians licensed in each.
Can a business decide what a member is prescribed?
No. The decision belongs to the clinician who evaluated that specific patient, and it has to be capable of being no. A program that pressures that decision is the problem the structure exists to prevent.
Who dispenses the medication?
A licensed pharmacy, shipping directly to the patient. For compounded preparations that is typically a 503A pharmacy compounding against a specific prescription for an identified patient, or a 503B outsourcing facility.
