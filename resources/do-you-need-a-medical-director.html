Do you need a medical director to offer a wellness program?

It depends on what your business is. For a licensed practice a medical director is often required. For a gym or a brand with no prescriber, hiring one does not create permission it did not have.

By the Pharity team Reviewed October 3, 2026 General information, not legal advice

The direct answer. If you are a medical practice or a med spa performing medical procedures, you likely need a medical director and your state will tell you so. If you are a gym, studio or brand with no prescribing capability, a medical director does not convert your business into one that may prescribe, and treating it as a workaround is a common and expensive mistake.

What a medical director actually does

The role is clinical oversight: standing orders and protocols, supervision and delegation where a state permits it, training and competency, adverse event handling, and accountability for the quality of care delivered under their licence. It is a working relationship with real duties, not a name on a wall.

That last point matters more than any other in this article. A director who has never seen your protocols, cannot name your staff and does not review anything is a liability dressed as a solution, and it is the arrangement that attracts attention when something goes wrong.

When one is genuinely required

Requirements are state specific and they change, so the only reliable answer comes from your own state board and your own counsel. As a general pattern, oversight is expected where a business performs medical procedures, where non-physician staff work under delegated authority, or where a corporate-practice restriction requires the clinical side to sit with a licensed professional.

What it cannot do

  • It does not let a lay business prescribe. Prescribing is personal to the licensed clinician who evaluates the patient.
  • It does not let a lay business dispense. Dispensing requires a pharmacy licence held by a facility.
  • It does not cure a corporate-practice problem. In many states the issue is who owns and directs the practice, and a director employed by a lay company can make that worse rather than better.
  • It does not make a per-prescription payment lawful. If money moves with the number of prescriptions, the arrangement is a problem whatever the oversight looks like.

The question that actually decides your path

Not "should we hire a medical director" but "does a licensed clinician inside our business evaluate and prescribe for patients".

If yes, you are a practice. The oversight question is real and your counsel should answer it, and the clinic route is the right one for you.

If no, you are a lay business. The right structure is not to acquire clinical capability but to stay cleanly on the marketing side, with the clinicians supplied by a professional entity that owns them, and with your own revenue coming from services you actually deliver at a flat price.

A practical test

Ask who would be named if a member had a bad outcome and asked who decided they should receive treatment. If the honest answer involves anyone in your business, the structure is wrong regardless of what the contract says. If the honest answer is a licensed clinician who evaluated that member independently and could have declined, the structure is sound.

This article is general information. Medical direction requirements are set by state law and vary considerably, so take your own counsel on your own facts before relying on any of it.

Common questions

Does a gym need a medical director?
A gym that performs no medical procedures and employs no clinicians generally has nothing for a medical director to direct. The more useful structure is for the clinicians to sit inside a professional entity owned by a physician, separate from the gym, rather than for the gym to acquire oversight it cannot use.
Does a med spa need a medical director?
Usually yes, where it performs medical procedures or uses delegated authority, and the requirement is set by state law. This is a question for your state board and your own counsel rather than a vendor.
Can a medical director let our staff prescribe?
No. Prescribing authority is personal to a licensed prescriber who evaluates the patient. Delegation rules differ by state and never extend prescribing to unlicensed staff.
Can we pay a medical director per prescription?
No. Compensation tied to the volume or value of prescriptions is the arrangement that fee-splitting and anti-kickback rules exist to prevent. Oversight is normally compensated at fair market value for time.
